## 1 · THE REVISED WEDGE

v1 positioned FCC as "a capital-allocation discipline engine" with a simulation MVP. v1.1 sharpens the wedge per the stage-2 review:

**FCC is the public decision-accountability protocol for on-chain capital.**

The product is not a portfolio, a dashboard, or a fund. The product is a *protocol* — a fixed, published way that a material capital decision becomes a permanent, auditable, challengeable object before its outcome is known. The institutional doctrine under evaluation (candidate language, §10): **Before capital moves, the decision enters the record.** Everything else in this blueprint — simulation, treasury mandate, tooling revenue — is a consumer of that protocol, not the product itself.

## 2 · THE CAPITAL INSTRUMENT — atomic unit, fully specified

**What exactly is a Capital Instrument?** A permanent, structured, hash-anchored record of one material capital decision, machine-readable (JSON, forecasts.json lineage) with a rendered human page, containing at minimum: decision · thesis · evidence chain (published-source links only) · risk statement (how this fails and what is lost) · position/sizing under Doctrine where applicable · explicit horizon · falsifiable resolution criteria fixed at filing · benchmark selections (from the Doctrine menu, §5) · counter-thesis (the standing adversarial annex, §4) · challenges and FCC responses (appended annexes) · SHA-256 of the locked body · timestamp + OTS anchor · final resolution · benchmark result · corrections history. An instrument makes the decision auditable **from before the outcome was known** — that ordering is the whole point, and it is enforced mechanically, not by promise.

**Lifecycle and lock — the state machine.** `DRAFT → FILED/LOCKED → OPEN → TERMINAL`. DRAFT (internal, no standing) → **FILED/LOCKED** (single event: the immutable body — decision, thesis, evidence list, risk, sizing, horizon, criteria, benchmarks — is hashed, timestamped, and OTS-anchored) → OPEN (challenge window runs; annexes accrue) → **TERMINAL** (exactly one terminal disposition, permanent): **RESOLVED-CORRECT** · **RESOLVED-INCORRECT** · **CONCEDED** · **VOID** · **INVALIDATED-BY-DOCTRINE**. Reaching TERMINAL includes the resolution work itself — criteria evaluated against the pre-named sources, benchmark results computed, open challenges resolved — recorded as annexes; there is no separate "resolved" waypoint between OPEN and the disposition. Post-terminal annotation happens only through corrections entries.

**RESOLVED-DISPUTED — determined NOT required.** The existing meta-challenge mechanism (§3: one round, dissent preserved) attaches disputes to *resolutions of challenges*, and its residue is a preserved dissent, not a different outcome. Making "disputed" a terminal disposition would let a dispute blur the outcome record — an instrument could terminate as neither correct nor incorrect, which is a manipulation surface pre-registration exists to remove (FCC could dispute its own misses into a gray zone). Instead, an unresolved dispute sets a **DISPUTED flag** on the instrument: the terminal disposition still evaluates strictly against the pre-registered criteria; the flag, the dissent annex, and a published count of disputed-flag instruments make the disagreement permanently visible without softening the disposition. No new terminal state is invented.

**When does an instrument become LOCKED?** At filing — lock and filing are the same event. The ordering rule that gives lock its meaning: **the lock timestamp must precede any action or price the instrument depends on.** For real capital (Stage 1+), the on-chain action must postdate the anchor. For any future simulated instrument (Stage 0.5), the entry price timestamp must postdate the anchor. An instrument whose dependent action predates its lock is invalid at birth (INVALIDATED-BY-DOCTRINE below). Back-dating is not deterred by policy; it is *detectable* by anyone holding the hash and the anchor — the same verification model as the corrections snapshots.

**What prevents editing after the outcome begins becoming known?** Three mechanisms, none of them trust: (i) the locked body's hash + Bitcoin-anchored timestamp are published — any byte change breaks public verification; (ii) the record is append-only — nothing in the locked body is ever rewritten; every later addition is a separately dated, separately hashed annex, visually distinct from the locked body, with the original hash re-displayed alongside; (iii) resolution criteria must name their data sources at lock, third-party-verifiable wherever possible, so "reinterpreting" the criteria has no room to operate. This is Dossier's permanence machinery applied to decisions.

**What can be amended after lock, and how is the amendment exposed?** Only annexes: challenge filings, FCC responses, the AI counter-thesis, evidence-defect corrections (each requiring a simultaneous FCC corrections-log entry), the resolution block, benchmark results, and post-resolution notes. What can never be amended: thesis, criteria, horizon, benchmarks, sizing — the pre-registration itself. If the pre-registration is discovered to be defective, the instrument does not get fixed; it terminates (below) and, if warranted, a successor instrument is filed fresh. Every annex carries its own timestamp and appears under an explicit AMENDMENT/ANNEX header; nothing is ever silently merged into locked text.

**The five terminal dispositions.** All permanent, all counted in FCC's public statistics (nothing is excluded from the record for being embarrassing): **RESOLVED-CORRECT** — criteria met at horizon per the pre-named sources; **RESOLVED-INCORRECT** — the ordinary honest failure; **CONCEDED** — an upheld challenge proves a flawed premise before the horizon; FCC terminates early against itself with credit to the challenger; a concession is a loss-class outcome, not an exit; **VOID** — resolution criteria become genuinely unmeasurable (a named data source ceases to exist), declared with explanation, forecast-spec lineage; **INVALIDATED-BY-DOCTRINE** — a filing-time violation is discovered (sizing breach, benchmark off-menu, ordering violation, undisclosed conflict). Evidence fraud anywhere triggers the integrity kill criterion (§9), not merely instrument invalidation.

**Scoring — two independent dimensions.** Every instrument is scored on two axes that never substitute for each other:
**OUTCOME** — did the underlying judgment resolve correctly against its pre-registered criteria and benchmarks? Values: CORRECT / INCORRECT / UNSCORED.
**PROCESS** — did FCC comply with Doctrine: disclosure, ordering, evidence, conflict, benchmark, and challenge-response requirements? Values: COMPLIANT / VIOLATED.
A correct outcome never erases a process failure — RESOLVED-CORRECT with a discovered violation publishes as OUTCOME = CORRECT · PROCESS = VIOLATED, both visible on the instrument and both counted. A compliant process never converts an incorrect outcome into a success — RESOLVED-INCORRECT under clean process publishes as OUTCOME = INCORRECT · PROCESS = COMPLIANT.
How the dispositions populate the two dimensions:
— **RESOLVED-CORRECT** → OUTCOME = CORRECT; PROCESS scored independently.
— **RESOLVED-INCORRECT** → OUTCOME = INCORRECT; PROCESS scored independently.
— **CONCEDED** → OUTCOME = INCORRECT (a concession is counted as a miss in every outcome statistic); PROCESS scored independently — and ordinarily COMPLIANT, because conceding when proven wrong is the challenge system *working*. The two-dimension design exists precisely so a concession can be an outcome miss and a process success at the same time.
— **VOID** → OUTCOME = UNSCORED (excluded from hit-rate numerator and denominator, since no fair evaluation exists) but never hidden: the VOID count is published adjacent to every hit-rate figure, and a rising VOID rate is itself a first-class published statistic. PROCESS scored independently — Doctrine's source-selection requirements apply, so a predictably fragile source can make a VOID instrument PROCESS = VIOLATED.
— **INVALIDATED-BY-DOCTRINE** → PROCESS = VIOLATED by definition; OUTCOME = UNSCORED for official statistics, with one anti-gaming rule: where the pre-registered criteria remain measurable at the horizon, the informational outcome is still computed and published on the instrument and in a separate published count — so invalidation can never be used to make a looming miss disappear.
Aggregate-statistics rule: every instrument appears in exactly one terminal-disposition count; outcome hit-rates are computed over scored outcomes (CORRECT + INCORRECT, with CONCEDED counted as INCORRECT); the process-violation rate is computed over **all** instruments; VOID, INVALIDATED, and DISPUTED-flag counts are always displayed alongside hit-rates, never footnoted away.

**How are benchmarks chosen before lock?** Doctrine v0.1 publishes a **benchmark menu** before the first instrument exists: a do-nothing baseline per denomination class and a mechanical passive comparator per instrument class, each defined by rule (no discretion at filing). An instrument cites its menu entries at lock; off-menu benchmarks require a Doctrine amendment *first* (Council-visible, corrections-logged). The challenge taxonomy's "bad benchmark" category polices menu misapplication before resolution — which is exactly when benchmark games must be caught to matter.

## 3 · CHALLENGE — the defining primitive

**PROVE COMMAND WRONG.** v1 treated challenge as one of four interface verbs; v1.1 elevates it to a defining primitive of the protocol, co-equal with the instrument itself: an instrument that cannot be challenged before resolution is just publishing; an instrument that can be is accountability.

**How challenge works.** A challenge is filed against an OPEN instrument, strictly before resolution, in a structured format: exactly one defect category per filing — flawed premise · missing evidence · ignored risk · inappropriate sizing · bad benchmark · conflict of interest · manipulable resolution criterion · invalid assumption — plus the specific claim and supporting evidence. The challenge is hashed and appended as an annex. FCC must respond on the record within a response window fixed in Doctrine v0.1 (duration set there, not here). Challenge and response are both permanent.

**How challenges are resolved.** Each defect category gets published resolution logic in Doctrine v0.1 (illustratively: "missing evidence" is upheld if the absent item, once obtained, changes the instrument's Doctrine qualification; "manipulable criterion" is upheld if the criterion admits a resolution the thesis-holder can influence). Challenges resolve **UPHELD / PARTIALLY UPHELD / NOT UPHELD** at instrument resolution, or earlier where the defect is dispositive (triggering CONCEDED). FCC resolves challenges against that published logic; one round of meta-challenge against a resolution is permitted (no infinite regress), and an unresolved dispute sets the instrument's DISPUTED flag (§2) — the dissent preserved in the annex, visible forever, without altering the terminal disposition. The honesty backstop: a challenge FCC dismissed that the outcome later vindicates is logged as an FCC process miss on FCC's own corrections log. Challenge resolution is itself part of FCC's scored record.

**Who may challenge?** Anyone, under a persistent pseudonymous identity (wallet signature or account). No token requirement — the standing no-token-gate principle extends to FCC accountability functions by default. Paying to hold power to account would invert the product.

**Spam and Sybil resistance without gambling.** No financial staking of any kind (guardrail). Instead: the structured format is itself the cost (malformed filings are returned unscored under published triage rules — returned, never silently deleted); one challenge per defect category per identity per instrument; duplicate defects consolidate with credit to the first filer; reputation (below) accrues only on UPHELD/PARTIALLY UPHELD outcomes and visibly degrades on a pattern of frivolous filings; identities are persistent and reputation is soulbound and non-transferable, so Sybil farming restarts at zero and zero-reputation filings carry no ranking weight while still being answered under triage rules. Paid challenge bounties (the red-team pattern) are *deferred*: introduce selectively only after organic challenge dynamics are observed (A9 validation), so money never precedes measurement.

**Reputation without gambling.** A public, non-financial reputation system scored purely on challenge quality and calibration: upheld-rate, dispositive-challenge count, stance calibration once STANCE exists. Ranks are soulbound, non-purchasable, carry no yield, no financial benefit, no access gate — "worth nothing on purpose," the Founding Member NFT's honest design applied to expertise. The lore ranks (Recruit → Officer) become earned, not claimed.

## 4 · AI — BACKSTAGE, ADVERSARIAL, NEVER IN COMMAND

FCC is not an AI fund and is never marketed as an AI product. AI is the standing adversarial layer: **Every FCC decision has an adversary.** System label (candidate): **ADVERSARIAL REVIEW // AI + HUMAN.**

**What AI is allowed to do:** generate the structured counter-thesis annex on every instrument — checking, at minimum: missing evidence, circular reasoning, liquidity assumptions, counterparty risk, benchmark fairness, Doctrine violations, conflicts, sizing, resolution manipulability; lint proposals and drafts for Doctrine compliance before filing; compute benchmark results mechanically; verify evidence-link integrity; assemble resolution data from the pre-named sources. Every AI output is labeled as AI output, appended as an annex, and reviewable by humans and by the public exactly like any other annex.

**What AI is prohibited from doing:** filing an instrument; locking an instrument; resolving an instrument or a challenge; holding, moving, or authorizing capital in any amount at any stage; generating evidence (it may only cite verifiable published sources); producing unlabeled content anywhere in the record; auto-executing any action at Stage 1+. **AI assists. It never has capital authority.** No unique-invention claims are made for AI red-teaming (A7); the differentiation claimed is narrower and defensible: the adversarial annex is *mandatory, structural, and permanent* on every instrument, rather than a feature someone can toggle.

## 5 · STAGE 0 — THE SMALLEST USEFUL ZERO-CAPITAL PRODUCT

Per §5–6 of the review, simulation leaves the MVP and DAO customers leave the Stage-0 architecture. Stage 0 is four artifacts:

1. **CAPITAL DOCTRINE v0.1** — published for public comment: eligibility and exclusions (including the standing exclusion: FCC never files instruments on $GFOF or any Federation-issued asset), sizing and concentration rules, evidence thresholds, the benchmark menu, the decision procedure, conflict rules, challenge-resolution logic per defect category, response windows, amendment process (Council-visible), the Stage-0.5 and Stage-1 gate conditions, the reporting format fixed before any results, and the kill criteria (§9) — everything that must exist *before results exist* so no goalpost can move later.
2. **CAPITAL INSTRUMENT SPECIFICATION v0.1** — the schema, lifecycle, lock/ordering rules, annex rules, terminal states, and verification procedure of §2, published for comment (liquidation-spec pattern: spec before code).
3. **CAPITAL RECORD** — the live append-only record, with FCC's own corrections log and a filed publish-either-way pre-commitment as its first entry. Stage-0 instruments need no portfolio and no simulated P&L: they are **qualification-stance instruments** — Capital Assessments concluded as a falsifiable stance ("would / would-not qualify under Doctrine §X; predicted qualification-relevant observable Y by horizon H") — plus FCC's own **process instruments** (doctrine adoption, gate definitions, exclusion rulings, each filed through the same protocol so FCC's institutional decisions eat their own accountability first). These are judgment forecasts in the same legal shape as Dossier's existing forecast record — still listed for counsel (§8), but they do not present hypothetical *performance*, which is what the earlier counsel gate now protects.
4. **CHALLENGE PROTOCOL** — live from the first instrument, per §3.

**Why someone returns at Stage 0:** open instruments with countdowns are arguments with a deadline — you return to file the challenge before the window closes, to see whether Command conceded, to watch a resolution land against the record, and to watch your own challenge resolve. The return loop is adversarial suspense plus permanent credit, not content.

## 7 · STAGE 1 — TREASURY SELF-MANDATE, HARD-GATED

The Federation's own treasury remains the preferred first real-capital mandate — already public, already on-chain, blast radius the Federation's alone. But FCC software existing is not authorization. The pre-published gate chain, in order: **Doctrine published and ratified → minimum resolved-instrument track record met → adversarial review demonstrably operating (resolved challenges exist, AI + human) → Council/owner ratification of the specific mandate (scope, caps, automatic drawdown-suspension triggers, emergency-halt procedure) → legal review of the mandate structure and wrapper → treasury mandate granted, recorded as a corrections entry.**

**What must be fixed in Doctrine before results exist** (so success can never be defined after the fact): the track-record threshold and its composition rules (what counts as resolved, how concessions and voids count); the scoring and reporting format; the benchmark menu; the suspension and halt triggers; the challenge-resolution logic; and the kill criteria. Numerical thresholds are deliberately **not** invented in this blueprint — none has a defensible basis yet; each is set in Doctrine v0.1 with published rationale, before any instrument resolves.

## 9 · KILL CRITERIA — FCC MUST BE ABLE TO FAIL ITS OWN THESIS

FCC's falsifiable core thesis: *a published doctrine plus mandatory adversarial review produces capital judgment that beats mechanical baselines, in public, over a pre-stated window.* Dossier's precedent binds: when the measurement comes back against the thesis, the answer is halt-and-record, never spin. Kill events, each pre-committed in Doctrine v0.1 with its checkpoint and threshold fixed there before results exist:

- **Judgment failure:** at the Doctrine-defined evaluation checkpoint, FCC's OUTCOME record (scored dispositions, concessions counted as misses) fails to beat its own null baselines → halt, public measurement review, no new instruments until the review resolves (the 28.5% protocol, applied to ourselves on purpose). A clean PROCESS record does not excuse this; the dimensions never substitute.
- **Arena death:** the challenge protocol attracts no genuine external participation within the Doctrine-defined validation window despite the Doctrine-defined outreach obligations → the "public accountability" premise is unvalidated; FCC does not advance to Stage 0.5/1 on an empty arena — it retools once, publicly, or halts. Accountability theater with no audience is worse than nothing.
- **Integrity breach:** any discovered back-dating, hash manipulation, or evidence fabrication → immediate halt and full disclosure; no cure path inside FCC v1.
- **Regulatory block:** counsel determines a core element cannot proceed → the affected stage is cancelled, not creatively worked around; if Stage 0 itself is blocked, FCC halts.
- **Capture:** a Dossier-firewall violation (intelligence bent toward a desired allocation) that cannot be structurally corrected → halt.

"Kill" always means halt-and-record: a corrections entry, a public state, the record preserved. FCC is only worth building if it can end the way Dossier's Program 01 paused — visibly, on its own log.
